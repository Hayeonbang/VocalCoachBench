VocalCoachBench

Can audio-language models coach your singing like a vocal trainer?

Vocal coaching asks more of a model than hearing and describing audio. A coach listens to the singer, finds what is wrong, decides what to fix first, and knows how to fix each problem. VocalCoachBench measures whether audio-language models can do this, against 18 professional vocal trainers on 515 recordings. So far they spot broad problem areas, but rarely the specific issue a coach would fix first.

01Dataset design

1

Scores are not enough

Scores failed in pilots. Trainers apply different thresholds to the same scale.

So we annotate coaching actions: identify · diagnose · prescribe · compare · localize.

2

Two subsets for two needs

Same-song subset207

One song, Amazing Grace, for everyone. Takes can be ranked against each other.

Diverse-song subset308

Different songs from nine public datasets. Varied styles and registers, closer to real coaching.

3

One protocol per recording

Per trainer: listen to the full take → review in issue-tagged blocks → rank the Top-3 issues.

Same-song adds a ranked triplet, because takes of one song are directly comparable. Diverse-song adds problem regions marked on the waveform, because with different songs the question is where the problem is, not who sang better. Two trainers per recording.

4

From reviews to evaluable targets

Reviews → atomic claims: diagnosis, correction (linked to its issue), strength.

Structured targets are scored deterministically. Free-form feedback is scored claim by claim, since trainers prioritize differently.

Details
  • Denominator. All 515 recordings (same-song 207, diverse-song 308), strict_all_attempts: malformed or missing outputs count as errors.
  • Top-3. Seven-class macro-F1 over 1,064 expert references, with each recording given equal total weight. PHONATION and the legacy internal label VOCALIZATION denote the same class.
  • Judge. openai/gpt-oss-120b: diagnosis = strict hit / coarse hit / miss / contradict; correction = valid / weak / invalid. Simple-protocol reviews are first decomposed into claims by the same model.
  • Protocols. Main = structured JSON prompts; Simple = short prompts with a distinct extraction protocol. Open/Closed identifies model access, not a separate evaluation protocol. The majority baseline sees no audio and has only a Top-3 metric.
  • Ranking. Score-derived and Direct pairwise accuracy are separate tasks: 189 same-song triplets, 567 pair decisions, 1,134 expert-reference attempts. Audio Flamingo 3 and Music Flamingo Direct use concatenated audio; Covo Direct is N/A.
  • Other denominators. Segment: 262. Diagnosis: 6,162 expert claims. Prescription validity: 2,575 fixed slots (515 × 5), including missing slots as failures; not validity conditional on emitted prescriptions.
  • Claims policy. Corrected515 format-reported: empty, unparseable or backend-error outputs retain zero claims and remain in the fixed denominator. See per-model notes for generation and parsing exceptions.
